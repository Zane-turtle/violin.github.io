Five Centuries of Craft

A shape perfected in the Renaissance.

The violin arrived at its iconic arching and outline in 16th-century Italy and has stood unchanged ever since.

c. 1550

Cremona, Italy

Andrea Amati establishes the four-string violin form — arched top and back plates, f-holes, and a curved bridge that modern luthiers still follow.

1666 – 1737

The Stradivari Workshop

Antonio Stradivari refines the golden-ratio outline and varnish chemistry across six decades; hundreds of his instruments remain among the most coveted on earth.

1698 – 1744

Giuseppe Guarneri "del Gesù"

A rival Cremonese master whose bolder carving created deep, explosive sonic power — favored by legendary virtuosi such as Niccolò Paganini.

19th Century

Symphonic Evolution

Longer fingerboards, steeper neck angles, and higher bridge heights are introduced to project sound across immense concert halls.

Present Day

Handcrafted Living Tradition

Master luthiers still carve spruce tops and maple backs with gouges and thumb planes, tuning each plate by finger tap and acoustic ear.
